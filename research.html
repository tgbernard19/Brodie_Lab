Research

From Genes to Watersheds

We combine molecular biology, advanced sensing, and computational modeling to understand and predict how microbial communities function, from individual root systems to whole watersheds.

DOE Science Focus Area

Watershed Function

The Watershed Science Focus Area (SFA) is an integrated, multi-laboratory Department of Energy project focused on the interacting impacts of disturbance and drought on the hydro-biogeochemical functioning of mountainous watersheds. Centered on the East River, Colorado watershed, this research aims to project the resistance and resilience of watersheds to future disturbance — from how microbes respond to drought to how genomes-scale traits scale up to control landscape-level water and nutrient fluxes.

DOE Watershed SFA — Full Overview

The Watershed SFA integrates diverse environmental data — from microbial genomics to plant ecology to remote sensing data - to develop a predictive "Genomes-to-Watershed" framework forecasting how key minerals and nutrients are retained or lost under different conditions.

Drought Effects on Soil Microbiomes

Which microbes are most drought-resilient in our forest soils? Are microbial functional processes in soil resilient to drought? Using genome-resolved metagenomes and metatranscriptomes, we are uncovering answers to inform the future of the watershed under increasingly frequent and severe drought.

NEON Campaign: Scaling Microbial Traits to Watershed Traits

The DOE NEON campaign in the East River watershed integrates high-resolution airborne remote sensing with intensive ground-based sampling of plants, soils, and microbes to scale hydro-biogeochemical processes from local hillslope measurements to the entire mountainous landscape.

Ecology & Agriculture

Soil Health

We seek to investigate the central role of soil biodiversity in supporting soil multi-functionality, ecosystem services, and the benefits provided to humanity. Our research integrates biochemical costs into microbial community assembly, elucidates plant-microbiome feedbacks and interactions, and ultimately leverages fundamental insights for translation into policy and technology.

Soil Biodiversity in California Agriculture

Prepared by the Belowground Biodiversity Advisory Committee (BBAC) for the California Department of Food and Agriculture (CDFA), this report provides the first strategic framework in the U.S. for measuring, monitoring, and integrating soil biodiversity into agricultural state programs — protecting essential ecosystem services and combating climate change.

Microbially-Enhanced Biochar for Soil Carbon Storage

This project develops a modular nature-based solution that uses biochar infused with whole-microbiome transplants to rapidly restore degraded soil and accelerate carbon storage. Funded by the Energy Biosciences Institute (EBI-Shell).

DOE Genomic Sciences

Biogeochemical Cycles

Our researchers investigate the biogeochemical processes that govern the flow of carbon, nitrogen, and other critical nutrients through soils and the rhizosphere. By linking microbial dynamics to hydrological and thermal conditions, we seek to understand how water movement, temperature, and microbial metabolism interact to control ecosystem function across scales — from individual root systems to entire watersheds. We combine field measurements, laboratory experimentation, and multi-omics approaches with computational modeling to translate mechanistic insights into improved predictive models.

Volatile Organic Compounds and Soil Carbon Storage

This DOE Genomic Sciences project investigates the overlooked role of Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) as a significant component of the soil carbon pool. Beyond their direct contribution, we predict that VOCs also act as signaling molecules that tether plants and microbes together — indirectly influencing carbon storage through biological "teleconnections." Because of their unique mobility, VOCs can travel between separated soil regions, and we are testing whether this allows them to be converted into stable carbon pools more effectively than non-volatile compounds.

Theory & Simulation

Microbial Theory

By linking microbial genomes and traits to dynamic energy-budget (DEB) and genomes-to-ecosystems (MicroTrait, G2E) models, our work turns "who is there" in complex communities into quantitative predictions of microbial fitness, interactions, and ultimately realistic predictions of ecosystem function and biogeochemical cycling. We leverage thermodynamic approaches to understand how power, efficiency, and entropy drive microbial evolution and adaptation.

Evaluating a Thermodynamic Theory of Microbiome Adaptation, Assembly & Evolution

This project evaluates a thermodynamic theory of microbiome assembly and evolution by quantifying power, efficiency, and entropy in microbial "dissipative structures." Using a novel modular platform that combines microfluidics with nanocalorimetry, we create controlled, observable microbial landscapes to test existing theories of how microbial communities form and change. We integrate these measurements with Dynamic Energy Budget (DEB) model development and genome-scale trait prediction, linking fundamental energetic principles to the genomic traits that govern microbial behavior.

EMERGE — Exascale Epidemiological Modeling

The EMERGE (ExaEpi for Elucidating Multiscale Ecosystem Complexities for Robust, Generalized Epidemiology) project is a $12 million multi-institutional initiative led by Berkeley Lab to develop a generalized, exascale-ready tool for predicting the spread of infectious diseases. Supported by NSF Microbiome Interactions and Mechanisms and DOE BRaVE.

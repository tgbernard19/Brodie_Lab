Research Areas

Our Science

We work across four interconnected areas, combining molecular biology, advanced sensing, and computational modeling to understand how microbial communities function from genes to entire watersheds.

DOE Science Focus Area

Watershed Function

Developing a predictive "Genomes-to-Watershed" framework that integrates microbial genomics and bedrock geophysics to forecast how mountainous watersheds retain and release water, carbon, and nutrients in a changing climate.

Explore project →

Ecology & Agriculture

Soil Health

Advancing crop and ecosystem resilience by investigating plant-fungal partnerships, microbial community assembly in the rhizosphere, and translating fundamental insights into policy and technology.

Explore project →

DOE Genomic Sciences

Biogeochemical Cycles

Investigating how carbon, nitrogen, and other nutrients flow through soils and the rhizosphere — from Volatile Organic Compounds as chemical signals to multi-omics approaches in high-altitude watersheds.

Explore project →

Theory & Simulation

Microbial Theory

Leveraging thermodynamic approaches and AI to understand how power, efficiency, and entropy drive microbial evolution — linking genomes and traits to ecosystem-scale predictions.

Our Approach

How We Work

Molecular Biology

Multi-omics approaches — metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, metabolomics — to characterize the structure and function of complex microbial communities at unprecedented resolution.

Advanced Sensing

Field campaigns, airborne remote sensing, and high-resolution environmental monitoring from individual root systems to entire mountainous watersheds.

Computational Modeling

Genome-informed trait-based models, dynamic energy budget frameworks, and AI-assisted tools that translate mechanistic insights into predictive ecosystem models.
